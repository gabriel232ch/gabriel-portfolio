## Context

A growing marketplace has to decide where to commit commercial attention and where operating reliability needs repair first. A single growth figure cannot settle that allocation question. This analysis reads scale, delivery and market exposure together.

The comparison is January–August 2017 against January–August 2018. Matching the period keeps the headline comparison from mixing a partial year with a full year. Delivered GMV is used as a proxy for marketplace value, not as a measure of profit.

## Analysis

The curated results show delivered GMV proxy rising from R$2.99M to R$7.22M. The underlying analysis reports 141.13% growth and allocates 99.40% of the change to order volume. Those percentages come from the underlying calculation, not from recomputing the rounded display values.

Over the same comparison, on-time delivery falls from 96.50% to 92.27%. Reading these signals together changes the decision question: the issue is not simply how to grow, but where growth can be supported without overlooking deteriorating service.

The Grow / Defend / Fix / Investigate structure makes that distinction visible. Six material Fix markets account for 2,920 orders and approximately R$409K in GMV exposure. The evidence supports attention to those markets; it does not turn all exposure into a recoverable opportunity.

## Limits

Growth attribution describes how the observed change is allocated; it does not prove a causal effect. GMV proxy is not revenue, contribution margin or incremental profit. A delivery association does not establish what any particular intervention would achieve.

The four portfolios are an explainable prioritization framework, not a forecast or a substitute for commercial judgment. The underlying project contains the full definitions and decision logic. This case study retains the already-curated findings without inventing segment-level forecasts or realized business impact.
